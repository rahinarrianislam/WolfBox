2
The idea

One persistent box per student,
for the whole degree.

LEARN

Coding tutor

Runs your code in an environment that matches the autograder. Gives hints, not answers, based on your history.

HELP

TA handoff

Notices when you've been stuck too long. Hands your TA a one-page summary of what you've tried.

PLAN

Registration planner

Knows your transcript and preferences. Builds conflict-free schedules and tracks remaining requirements.

It sleeps when you're away, wakes in ~1 second, and keeps everything in /data.

Plan: registration & schedules

Registration without the spreadsheet.

  • Remembers you: transcript, completed requirements, preferences like "no 8 AMs" or "I work Tue/Thu afternoons", max credits.
  • Generates 3 schedule options with no conflicts, ranked by your preferences, and explains the trade-offs.
  • Checks prereqs and shows what's left for your degree. The 4-year plan updates each semester.
  • Section full? Suggests the next-best swap that keeps the rest of your week intact.
  • Reminds you before your enrollment window opens.

A real solver builds the schedules. Gemma 4 reads your preferences in plain English and explains the options, so it can't invent sections.

5
Why this wins

One project, three tracks.

MARITIME

Persistence is the product

  • Agent box per student, kept in /data
  • Runs student code isolated from other students
  • Sleeps when idle, wakes in ~1s
  • Each box costs ~$1/month, so it scales
GEMMA 4

The brain

  • Hints that use your history
  • TA summaries
  • Plain-English schedule preferences
  • Through the Gemini API
OPEN-SOURCE AI

Open weights

  • Gemma 4 counts as open-weight
  • We open-source the repo
  • Schools could self-host the whole thing

Judging fit: clear impact (every judge was a student) · creative (memory is the twist) · realistic (~$1/student/month) · easy to demo.

7
The demo (~3 minutes)

Show the memory, not the chat.

  1. Meet Alex, a student whose box is pre-seeded with "3 weeks" of history. Box is asleep; ask a question; it wakes in ~1s.
  2. Alex submits buggy hw4. WolfBox links the bug to the hw2 mistake.
  3. Side by side: a fresh student asks the same question and gets a generic hint. That's why persistence matters.
  4. Alex hits the stuck threshold and gets a nudge. Click through to the TA summary.
  5. Switch to Plan: "no 8 AMs, I work Tue/Thu", which returns 3 schedules and what's left for the degree.

Decide before we code

Open questions.

  • Stack: Python (FastAPI) or TypeScript for the box and orchestrator? Maritime has SDKs for both.
  • Course: which assignment do we fake? A tiny Python/Java homework with ~5 unit tests.

Out of scope today
